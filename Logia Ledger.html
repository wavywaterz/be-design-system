1 · -logia, the suffix itself

Greek logos — word, account, reason.

Every "-logy" simply means an account of something: someone looked carefully, for a long time, and wrote down what held true. Be's whole practice is handing people those accounts one at a time, in plain words. The ledger below is the shelf they come from.

2 · Biology — the study of living things

bios (life) + logia — an account of life.

In plain words: what bodies are and do — cells, breath, sleep, growth, each other. Why Be cares: it's the namesake. Be exists to remind beings that they are biology first: an organism with a clock, a breath, and a need for others — not a profile with a body attached.

3 · Ecology — how living things hold together

oikos (house, home) + logia — an account of the household.

In plain words: no living thing survives alone; ecology studies the web — who feeds whom, who depends on whom. The bee is its plainest teacher.

About 75% of the world's food-crop types depend on animal pollinators at least in part, and roughly 35% of global crop production (by volume) is touched by them — pollination that FAO values at over $200B a year. Honest footnote: the wind-pollinated staples (wheat, rice, corn) don't need bees; what we'd lose is most of the vitamin-rich, colorful food. FAO "Why bees matter" · Our World in Data · U.S. FWS

Client-safe phrasing: "one in three bites involves a pollinator" — vivid and roughly right; say "types of crops" when you use the 75% figure.

4 · Ethology — how animals behave (the bees)

ethos (character, custom) + logia — an account of character.

In plain words: the study of behavior in the wild — not what animals are made of, but what they do. Why Be cares: the two bees in the logo are an ethology lesson: harmony isn't sameness, it's coordination.

A returning forager bee performs a "waggle dance" that encodes direction and distance to flowers; the better the find, the longer she dances. Nestmates compare dances and the colony settles on the best patch — a group decision with no leader, made of many small honest reports. Karl von Frisch decoded this (Nobel, 1973); modern work still refines when colonies lean on it. Communications Biology (2024) · Seeley, Honeybee Democracy

This is Be's model of togetherness: share what you found, plainly; let the circle decide; no central boss.

5 · Chronobiology — the body's clocks

chronos (time) + bios + logia — an account of life's timing.

In plain words: bodies run on schedules — sleep, temperature, hormones — set daily by light. Why Be cares: "morning light before screens" is the app's oldest act.

People living under electric light drift later and get less daytime sun; a week of camping with only natural light re-synced their internal clocks to the sun — biological night began at sunset again. A weekend outdoors achieved about 69% of that shift. Wright et al., Current Biology, 2013 · follow-up 2017

6 · Neurobiology — naming feelings

neuron (nerve) + bios + logia — an account of the nervous system.

In plain words: the brain's alarm (amygdala) fires before words arrive. Putting the feeling into words changes what the brain is doing. Why Be cares: it's the "Comprehend" step of the Charge protocol.

In fMRI, labeling an emotion ("that's anger") dampened amygdala response to negative images and engaged the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex — naming quiets the alarm along a measurable pathway. Lieberman et al., Psychological Science, 2007

7 · Physiology — the breath

physis (nature) + logia — an account of how the body works.

In plain words: some body dials are hand-reachable; breath is the biggest one. Long exhales lean on the body's brake. Why Be cares: it's the "Manage" step — five breaths, exhale longer.

In a month-long randomized study, five minutes daily of exhale-weighted "cyclic sighing" improved mood and lowered resting respiratory rate more than mindfulness meditation. Honest footnote: modest scale (~100 people), self-reported mood. Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023

8 · Epidemiology — togetherness, counted

epi + demos (upon the people) + logia — an account of what happens to populations.

In plain words: zoom out from one body to thousands and patterns appear that no single life can show. Why Be cares: it's the proof that the Hive isn't decoration — company is a health behavior.

Across 148 studies and 308,849 people, stronger social relationships meant a 50% higher likelihood of survival over the study periods — an influence on mortality comparable to smoking, and larger than inactivity or obesity. Holt-Lunstad et al., PLoS Medicine, 2010
The body's channels of togetherness are measurable too: consensual touch eased pain, depression, and anxiety across 137 studies of ~13,000 people; a hug on a conflict day softened the mood dip into the next day (404 adults, correlational); and shared laughter upregulates the endorphin system that bonds primate groups — a giggle is grooming at group scale. Packheiser et al., Nature Human Behaviour, 2024 · Murphy et al., PLOS ONE, 2018 · Dunbar, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B, 2022

9 · Bioorganic chemistry — the molecules

bios + organon (instrument) + chemistry — life's chemistry, molecule by molecule.

In plain words: under every feeling is chemistry — melatonin rising at dusk, cortisol with stress, oxytocin with warm contact. Why Be cares: it grounds the language ("your body makes its own sleep signal") — but Be names molecules sparingly and never promises to "boost" any of them; molecule talk slides into supplement talk fast. No specific claims cited here by design — when a claim is needed, it gets its own source first.

10 · Permaculture — ecology, applied

permanent + culture — coined by Mollison & Holmgren, late 1970s.

In plain words: designing human systems the way healthy ecosystems already work. Its three classic ethics — earth care, people care, fair share — read like Be's consent principles wearing boots. Why Be cares: waste reduction as wellness: nothing wasted, not even a difficult feeling; outputs of one act feed the next (a walk is light + movement + company at once).

11 · Technology — the craft of tools

techne (craft, skill) + logia — an account of craft.

In plain words: technology is the account of how tools are made — including gentle ones. Be sits in the "calm technology" lineage (Weiser & Brown, 1990s): a tool should sit at the edge of attention and only step forward when invited. Why Be cares: it's why the app is offline-first, opt-in everything, no streaks, no countdowns — the tool defers to the being.

12 · How to use this in conversation

  • Lead with the act, not the field: "ten minutes of morning light" lands; "chronobiology" is the footnote if they ask why.
  • One finding at a time — the same rule as the app. A person handed three studies keeps none.
  • Say the honest footnote out loud ("the evidence is early", "types of crops, not volume"). It builds more trust than the finding itself.
  • Never prescribe: findings are invitations. "May" and "tends to" over "will".
  • If asked something this ledger doesn't cover: "I don't want to guess — let me find the source." That sentence is the brand.
